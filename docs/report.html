Key takeaways

A named person, a deadline, and a fallback

Each empty box gets one person who is asked by name and has 2 hours to say yes. A refill nobody completes moves on automatically, and finally becomes an open call in the office channel.

Exactly one buyer, guaranteed

One open refill per office, and one commit per refill under a lock. The rule is tested with 8 threads racing, 20 times over. Removing the lock fails all 20. All 73 test methods (120 runs, including repeats and one layering check per layer) pass.

Fair, and money moves fast

Whoever did the fewest recent refills and is owed the least goes first. The HR claim is filed the moment the receipt arrives and retried if HR is down.

Budgets in each office's own currency, one total for management

6 000 NOK and 15 000 INR are checked in their own currencies, by each office's local calendar. Exchange rates are only used for the combined monthly total.

1. Problem statement

Two offices, each with a snack counter kept stocked by volunteers: about 15 in Oslo and about 20 in Bengaluru. When a box is empty, a volunteer buys snacks with their own money and submits the receipt to the company's HR system to be reimbursed. This runs on goodwill and informal messages, and it is breaking down:

  E([Box goes empty]) --> W{Who refills?}
  W -->|everyone assumes someone else will| D[Box sits empty for days]
  W -->|two people act on the same day| T[Snacks bought twice]
  W -->|the usual few step up again| F[Same volunteers pay again]
  T --> H[Receipt sent to HR by hand]

What we know

  • HR is run by another company. Claims carry amount, currency, description, receipt image, employee id. Approval takes 1 to 10 days, and employees are paid in their payroll currency.
  • Management wants one monthly report of total spend across both offices.
  • Employees sometimes visit the other office for a week or two.
  • Volunteers work hybrid: some days in the office, some at home, sometimes on holiday.
  • Everyone has a company login and uses the internal chat tool.

2. What "automate" means here

The coordination and the paperwork are automated, not the shopping. A person still buys the snacks, but the system decides who, makes sure only one person buys, checks the budget, files the HR claim and builds the report. The chat tool is the user interface, because everyone already has it.

Questions asked before building, and the answers

QuestionDecision
How do we pick who buys?Fairness order among volunteers present at that office today
How long before a refill moves on?2 hours to commit, 1 day to upload the receipt (configurable)
What happens when a purchase exceeds the budget?Not a hard stop: the office admin approves
Which currency is the combined report in?Each office in its own currency, plus a combined total in NOK
Can visitors refill the office they are visiting?Yes: that office's budget and currency, and HR pays them in their payroll currency
What shape should the code take?Spring Boot REST API, in memory, simulated chat and HR behind interfaces

5. Refill state machine

Every refill is in exactly one of 10 states. Each transition is guarded in the Refill entity, so an illegal jump throws an error instead of corrupting the data.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> REPORTED: box reported empty
  REPORTED --> AWAITING_BUDGET_APPROVAL: budget left below typical minimum
  REPORTED --> OFFERED: someone present to ask
  REPORTED --> NEEDS_VOLUNTEER: nobody present
  AWAITING_BUDGET_APPROVAL --> OFFERED: admin approves
  OFFERED --> COMMITTED: commit (asked person, or someone at that office stepping in)
  OFFERED --> OFFERED: 2 h passed or declined, ask next person
  OFFERED --> NEEDS_VOLUNTEER: nobody left to ask
  NEEDS_VOLUNTEER --> COMMITTED: anyone at the office commits
  COMMITTED --> OFFERED: no receipt in 1 day or declined
  COMMITTED --> READY_TO_CLAIM: receipt within budget
  COMMITTED --> AWAITING_ADMIN_APPROVAL: receipt above the spending limit
  AWAITING_ADMIN_APPROVAL --> READY_TO_CLAIM: admin approves
  READY_TO_CLAIM --> CLAIM_SUBMITTED: HR accepts the claim
  READY_TO_CLAIM --> READY_TO_CLAIM: HR down, retry every minute
  CLAIM_SUBMITTED --> REIMBURSED: HR approves
  CLAIM_SUBMITTED --> CLAIM_REJECTED: HR rejects, admin follows up
  REIMBURSED --> [*]
  CLAIM_REJECTED --> [*]
Box still empty
REPORTED, AWAITING_BUDGET_APPROVAL, OFFERED, NEEDS_VOLUNTEER, COMMITTED. A new "empty" report joins this refill.
Volunteer still owed money
AWAITING_ADMIN_APPROVAL, READY_TO_CLAIM, CLAIM_SUBMITTED. This pushes them down the "who buys" order.
Counts against the budget
Every state with a receipt except CLAIM_REJECTED.

  A[Refill needs a buyer] --> B[All registered volunteers]
  B --> C{At this office today?}

  C -->|yes, including visitors| D{Handed off already?}

Presence (where someone is today, not their home office) handles hybrid work, holidays and visitors. "Today" uses the office's local time.

7. Budget check: two checkpoints

Only the office's admins can approve; another office's admin gets 403. An exchange rate never decides whether snacks can be bought.

8. Deadlines and handover

flowchart TD
  S[Scheduler, every minute] --> L[Each OFFERED or COMMITTED refill]
  L --> D{Deadline passed?}
  D -->|no| K[Leave it]
  D -->|yes| H[Hand off the assignee: added to the handed-off list]
  H --> M{Had they committed?}
  M -->|yes| M1["Message: handed to someone else. Please don't buy."]
  M -->|no| M2[Message: offered to someone else]
  M1 & M2 --> N{Next candidate?}
  N -->|found, excluding handed-off people| O[OFFERED to them, new 2 h deadline]
  N -->|none| P[NEEDS_VOLUNTEER: anyone may commit, even earlier people]
  DEC[Volunteer declines] --> H

Running the check twice doesn't skip anyone: each refill is re-checked under its lock. A person who was handed off can't take the refill back while someone else is being asked, but can once it's open to everyone.

9. Only one buyer: concurrency

sequenceDiagram
  actor I as Ingrid
  actor L as Lars
  participant R as Refill 7 (lock per refill)
  par same moment
    I->>R: commit
  and
    L->>R: commit
  end
  Note over R: Only one thread holds the refill lock at a time
  R-->>I: OK, OFFERED becomes COMMITTED
  R-->>L: 409 Conflict: already being bought by Ingrid
  Note over R: Second safety net: the entity refuses COMMITTED to COMMITTED
Office lock: two "empty" reports at once still create a single refill (tested with 6 threads, 20 times).
Refill lock: check and act happen atomically, and the first commit wins (tested with 8 threads, 20 times).
Mutation check: with the lock removed, the test failed all 20 runs. Locks are always taken office first, then refill, so they can't deadlock.

11. Each problem and how it is solved

Problem in the briefHow it is prevented
The box sits empty for days because everyone assumes someone else will refill itOne named person is asked, deadlines are enforced (2 h / 1 day), the refill is handed on automatically, and the last resort is an open call in the channel. Status and history are visible.
Two people buy snacks on the same dayOne open refill per office, and one commit per refill (two locks plus a state guard). The channel is told who is buying. Anyone taken off a refill is told "don't buy".
A few people do most refills and wait weeks for their moneyFairness order (90-day window, unpaid claims, longest ago). The claim is filed when the receipt arrives and retried if HR is down. The report shows money still owed.
Nobody knows the monthly spend or which snacks get eatenSpend per office in its own currency, a combined monthly report, and top items from the receipts.
Hybrid work, holidays, visitorsVolunteers are picked by daily presence at an office. A visitor refills against the visited office's budget, and HR pays them in their payroll currency.
Two time zones"Today" and "this month" are per office. At 00:30 on 1 December, Bengaluru has its December budget while Oslo is still in November.

14. Trade-offs, and what is deliberately not built

Trade-offs

  • Budget in each office's own currency; exchange rates only for the report.
  • Admin approval, not a hard stop, when over budget.
  • Daily check-in for presence: simple, but people can forget, so the open call is the fallback.
  • Clock-time deadlines, not working hours: simpler, configurable.
  • Polling HR, not webhooks: it's another company's system.
  • Stepping in is allowed: faster refills, but it can bypass the fairness order.
  • In memory, one instance. For production: a database, a unique constraint for one open refill per office, and optimistic locking.

Deliberately not built

  • Receipt OCR: the volunteer types the amount, and HR checks the image.
  • Sensors or smart scales on the box.
  • Our own app or UI: chat is where people already are.
  • Per-snack consumption tracking.
  • Production chat and HR adapters, SSO, multiple instances.
  • Policy idea: a company card or supplier delivery would remove reimbursement altogether.

Summary

Delivered

Design document, working lifecycle, choosing who buys, budget check, simulated chat and HR, monthly report, REST API, and a demo script.

Proven

120 tests: one buyer even under a race, automatic handover, correct budgets and totals in NOK and INR, time-zone month boundaries, HR down or rejecting, and the layer boundaries.

Next steps

Real chat and HR adapters, persistent storage with database constraints, calendar or badge-based presence, and ask management about a company card.
